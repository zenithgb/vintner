# Phase 5F — Rootstock Strategy Acceptance

Rootstocks add an optional vineyard decision without making ordinary grape
cuttings obsolete. Vines planted from normal cuttings remain on their own
roots and continue to work exactly as before.

## Progression

1. Craft an **Adapted Rootstock Cutting** from either grape cutting and rooted
   dirt. This abstracts selecting vigorous local roots for difficult ground.
2. Craft a **Resistant Rootstock Cutting** from an adapted rootstock, compost,
   and nether wart. This represents deliberate selection under disease
   pressure rather than literal magical roots.
3. Insert either rootstock into a Nursery Bed and let it reach stage 3.
4. Hold a Grafting Knife in one hand and a red or white grape cutting in the
   other, then use the knife on the mature rootstock.
5. Plant the resulting grafted cutting on a trellis.
6. Use the Vintner's Almanac on the vine to see its root system.

## Strategy

- **Own roots:** no bonus; simplest and cheapest option.
- **Adapted roots:** improve vine health during nutrient imbalance, drought,
  and heat.
- **Resistant roots:** improve vine health during mildew and rot pressure, and
  provide a smaller nutrient-imbalance benefit.

Neither specialist rootstock is universally strongest.

## Manual acceptance

- [ ] Both rootstock items have valid inventory models and recipes.
- [ ] Each rootstock renders through four visible nursery growth stages.
- [ ] Empty-hand harvesting a mature rootstock produces three matching
  rootstock cuttings.
- [ ] Breaking an occupied bed returns the inserted rootstock cutting.
- [ ] Nursery grafting consumes one grape cutting and one knife durability in
  Survival, then empties the bed.
- [ ] The grafted cutting plants normally on any wood trellis.
- [ ] The Almanac reports Own Roots, Adapted Roots, or Resistant Roots.
- [ ] Adapted roots help drought/heat sites; resistant roots help wet,
  disease-prone sites.
